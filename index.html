Sunpatch

How much sun does this spot really get?

Every seed packet is written in hours of direct sun. Almost nobody knows what their own garden actually gets — so the tomatoes go in the shady corner and never ripen.

Your bed
3.4 hours of sun

The label said full sun — six or more. Your fence takes the rest.

Measure your own skyline

Other sun apps show you where the sun is. That is the easy half. The half that decides whether anything grows is what stands in the way. Point your phone at the top of the fence, the garage, next door's lime tree — or just enter how tall and how far — and Sunpatch builds the skyline around that exact spot.

Real hours, any day of the year

The app walks the day minute by minute and counts only the light that actually reaches you. You get the total, the first and last sun, the longest unbroken stretch, and the same figures for every day of the year — so you can see the bed that bakes in July sit in shadow from October.

Then it tells you what will grow

Full sun, part sun, part shade, full shade — the same bands the labels use. 135 plants matched against the light you actually have, and a plain list of what will not work.

No account. Nothing uploaded.

Every calculation runs on your phone. There is nothing to sign up for and it all works in aeroplane mode. Your location is used to place a spot and never leaves the device.
